The one sentence that reframes it

Four review rounds on PR #178 produced 31 findings, and most of them were defects in the guards the previous round added — not in the renderer those guards protect.

That is not an impression. It is attribution, measured with git show <sha>:scripts/gen-dashboard.py at each commit:

🟠 Finding A — the guard inventory cannot see a guard nothing calls

The repo has an inventory that enumerates every check-*.py and enforces a contract on it. Its docstring is explicit about why it does not use a registry:

check-ratchet-contract.py:68-72
    "Ratchet scripts, from TWO independent sources so neither alone can be evaded.
     A registry list would be evadable by simply not registering — the 'rule that
     depends on remembering' shape."

The two sources are (1) scripts named by a CI step in ci.yml, and (2) scripts whose own docstring self-declares as a ratchet. Both presuppose the guard is already wired or already labels itself. So:

$ python3 scripts/check-ratchet-contract.py
ratchets discovered (14): …
ratchet contract OK

14 discovered, of 24 on disk. And the contract enforces two rules — a --self-test exists, and no fail-open handler. It is honest that only 2 of its 6 rules are statically decidable. But "has a caller" is statically decidable and is not one of the six.

Why this is the finding and not a bug report

The class was named by Phase 6 #1, thirty-one days ago. check-arch-findings.py's own docstring describes that review's Finding #2 as:

"Finding #2 of that review is literally 'a correct module exists and nobody calls it.'"

A ratchet was built — for that instance. And check-schema-gates.sh records the same class three more times, in comments:

:52   "⟳ r4 BLOCKING (codex + claude): the live gate existed for a whole day
       and NOTHING CALLED IT."
:109  "check-anon-exposure.py was named in the roadmap and the plan and
       invoked by NOTHING."

Four recorded instances. Four instance-fixes. The class-check was never built — even though the inventory to build it on already exists and already globs every check-*.py at check-ratchet-contract.py:190.

🟢 Finding C — an unrun guard rots silently

check-plan-task-order.py:26 says # 12 cases. The suite reports 16/16. check-plan-code.py:694 has a count_drift mechanism for precisely this, applied to its own docstring and no other script's.

Low on its own — the script is unreachable anyway. It is here because it is the evidence for A: this is what a guard nobody runs looks like after a while.

No generator imports another's renderer. The sharp part is that the project already knows the right seam and wrote down why:

gen-dashboard.py:402-408
    "The dependency arrow points generator -> gate, never the reverse: a GATE
     must not import the thing it guards, but a page importing a gate is what
     keeps their readings identical by construction."

That mechanism is used for the entry grammar, and again at gen-backlog-page.py:74 for check-docs.py's split rule. It has never been used for rendering. Two comments in gen-backlog-page.py (:1432, :1715) observe that gen-dashboard.py had the same contrast holes — found separately, on a different day.

Deletion test. Delete _inline from gen-dashboard.py: complexity does not vanish, it reappears in three other generators — where it already partly lives. That is a module earning its keep with no seam to reach it through.

A refutation, recorded because I nearly filed it

I suspected check-paid-caller-arrival.py was a fifth orphan. grep for its filename in check-schema-gates.sh returned only comment lines, and dev-process.md claims it was wired as gate 15. Reading the file rather than grepping it shows:

check-schema-gates.sh:254
run "15/15 backlog 26's money trigger (no non-test caller reaches record_artifact)"

The gate is real and the doc's claim is true. A grep for the name missed a run line that describes the gate in prose — the same "searched for the mechanism, not the property" shape this whole milestone kept producing. Filed nowhere.

Three decisions nobody wrote down

The question dev-process.md says no tool can answer.

  • That the guard stack should be seven layers. No ADR, no spec sentence, no decision record. It accreted one round at a time, each layer justified by a real defect. Nobody chose the aggregate.
  • That --mutate . may execute delivered scripts against your real $HOME. Rounds 2–4 spent real effort making individual manifest entries safe under that decision. The decision itself — rather than running the whole harness under a redirected HOME — was never stated or examined.
  • That a truncated headline may be silently altered to balance its markup. Round 3 chose to append closers rather than cut back; round 4 found that choice fabricating text on your page. It is a decision about your prose and it lives only in a code comment.

The four candidates

2  recommended first

Make the inventory's population the filesystem, and add "has a caller" to the contract. Closes A, B and C together.

Small — the glob at check-ratchet-contract.py:190 already walks every check-*.py; the change is which set feeds the contract, plus one new statically decidable rule. Kills a class the project has paid for five times. Risk: some of the 10 currently undiscovered scripts may be deliberately unwired, so the rule needs an explicit opt-out with a written reason — the same shape as REVIEW GAP:.

1  the deepening opportunity proper

Give the inline renderer a seam. Closes D.

The larger structural win: the behaviour that took four rounds to harden currently lives hardened in one of the four places it exists. Wants a design conversation — what the module is called, what its interface is, and whether the generator→gate arrow is the right direction for a renderer or whether this wants a third thing both import.

3

Decide the $HOME question once, rather than per manifest entry.

Cheapest of the four and removes a whole hazard category rather than guarding instances of it. Arguably a prerequisite for trusting the mutation harness at all.

4

Flatten the verification stack — retire layers whose defect-finding rate no longer justifies their own defect rate.

The most valuable and the most dangerous. Every layer was added for a measured reason, so removal needs evidence per layer, not a general argument. I would not do this before 2 and 3, because both reduce the stack's cost without removing anything.
