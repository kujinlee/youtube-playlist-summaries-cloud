The prevention rules, ordered by leverage

⚠ These are prevention, not detection. An enumerated citing-sites list, a CLASS SWEPT: clause and an alternating round all find the defect after it is made. These aim to make it unrepresentable.

#RuleWhy it prevents rather than detects
1One owner per fact; everything else points.the citing-sites problem existed because “where §N lives” was written in seven places. With one owner there is no class to sweep, so an instance-not-class fix becomes impossible rather than discouraged
2Derive the number; never write it. Cite a run id, SHA or symbol, or have the producer print ita document inside the corpus it describes is stale at the commit that writes it. 7 of 19 findings were this — and 4 of my own defects yesterday were numbers I typed
3A fact in a document must have a PRODUCER or an OWNER. If it has neither, it is not written.rules 1 and 2 as a single test the author can apply at write time, with no tooling at all
4One purpose per commit.the reset --hard that destroyed round 3's testimony was only needed because a rename was bundled with content edits. Committing the rename alone first removes the need for the destructive repair entirely
5Make a stale reference unrepresentable — references resolved at build time, i.e. a generated index⚠ named honestly as the expensive one. The only rule here that requires building something — and the one that would retire rule 1's manual discipline

Which numbers must be exact — the discriminator the repo lacks

The cost of vagueness — the real objection to your proposal

A ball-park trades a detectable error for an undetectable one. “13” against an actual 15 is comparable — a reader or a gate can catch it. “About a dozen” when it is 40 is wrong in a way nothing can detect, because the claim never committed to anything.

So the rule is not be vague. It is: state the precision you can defend — and if a decision depends on the exact value, it needs a PRODUCER, not prose.

A live instance, which beats a cited one

We spent part of today re-deriving the remove-the-count decision from first principles, and neither of us found #183 or §26 until we went looking. That is number-populations' diagnosis happening in real time — “re-deriving, one instance at a time, a decision this repo had already made and filed where nothing points” — about numbers, between the two people discussing it.

Rules 1 and 3 are one rule seen from two ends

Your point that a repair should fix all instances rather than the one the previous round named is right — and note it is prevention, not detection: it prevents the next round's finding.

⭐ The sharper form: if you cannot enumerate the instances, that is the signal to de-duplicate rather than sweep harder — because a fact with one owner has exactly one instance. Fix every instance is what you do when duplication already exists; one owner per fact stops it existing.

What prevention cannot reach

Judging whether work is covered by an existing design is irreducibly a judgement, and nothing catches a wrong call before the fact. So thrashing-as-detection still has to exist. Prevention shrinks the surface; it does not remove the need for the signal.

So upfront and reactive are not properties of a review. They are properties of the relationship between a review and a piece of work.

That is why the two sentences above could sit in one document, pass a gate and survive three rounds without anyone noticing: they describe the same instrument from two vantage points. Nobody was wrong — the vocabulary could not hold the distinction.

Four consequences, stated so you can argue with them:

Consequence
1Architecture review is not a phase. It is an instrument producing a design in force over a scope. Running it again extends or redraws that scope. The record agrees — 14 exist, including two pairs filed on a single day.
2The question is never “should we review upfront?” but “is the work I am about to start covered by a design currently in force?”
3The art is judging scope coverage — irreducibly a judgement, which is why Q0's entry half is yours.
4The science is detecting that the judgement was wrong. Thrashing is that signal. It fires after the fact — its limitation, and also why it works: it needs no classifier.

5The guiding directive

Your framing: mixing architecture review and review rounds is an art rather than a science, but we need a guiding directive. Both are trial-and-error correction methods; they differ in the grain of error they can see, not in kind.

The directive

Before starting work, name the design in force over it.
If none is, that is the thing to produce first.
If you cannot name its scope, you are not covered.

⚠ Deliberately a directive and not a decision tree. A mechanical classifier for “is this a seam?” does not exist — three hand-kept path lists each scored something dangerous as one-round. The directive asks for what a person can answer and a reader can check: a name and a scope.

Grain of errorInstrument that sees itWhy the others cannot

a guard that cannot failthe mutation sweepa green suite proves nothing about a hollow falsifier

When a finding might have siblings, change instrument — don't run another round

You said all instances of one class should be found in one review, not serially across rounds. The honest constraint: a class is usually invisible from a single instance — it becomes visible at instance two, which is why rounds discover it serially.

But that argues for a different instrument, not for reviewing harder

Measured: a corpus run examined 5,287 real calls where six review rounds had examined ~7 hand-picked cases. So “find them all in one pass” is reachable — by switching instrument.

When a finding looks like it may have siblings, the next step is a CORPUS RUN, not round N+1.

⚠ And consolidation is not missing either — it arrives late by construction. “The three Blockings are not three mistakes; they are one mechanism defect” is an architecture review doing exactly the N-to-1 consolidation you describe. The defect is that it is convened only on thrashing — after N rounds have already been paid for.
