08The decision

Q1 — the mapping

Do goals (3)→D1, (2)→D2 hold, and where does goal (1) belong?

Goals (2) and (3) are not in doubt: (3) is the product, and (2) is the review method, the ratchets and the process documents — the exact class your 2026-08-11 statement reframed as a first-class deliverable rather than overhead. Goal (1) is the real question.

Recommended — D2, but only via its exported question

The suite makes this project legible, which on its face is D1 overhead. But D2's own rule is what travels is the question a tool asks, never the tool. A dashboard built for this repository will never be reused; “a person who was away can see what changed and what needs them, without reading the transcript” travels to any project. So the suite is D1 scaffolding whose question is the D2 contribution.

The strongest argument against

That reasoning would make everything D2, because every tool here asks some question. If D2 accepts a contribution this abstract, portable-practices.md fills with restatements and stops being a harness anyone could pick up.

The answer to it — two filters, and they must stay strict

Every D2 entry has to pass both: measured (files, numbers, output — not a confident belief), and project-independent (it would hold with no Supabase, no Gemini, no Fly). The abstraction does not get in on being abstract; it gets in on passing those two. Most of this project's best lessons fail the second filter, and that is the correct outcome — the answer is to leave them out, never to loosen the filter so the alignment looks tidier.

Q2 — the goal change

Does “overall comprehensibility suite” mean the goal change roadmap:2355 defers?

Option A — the three existing anchors, gathered under one heading

No goal change. Costs nothing, breaks nothing, and the anchors already exist and are already enforced by check-anchors. If you meant “show me these three together”, this is the whole job.

Option B — recommended if you mean it as a main goal: register it

Declaring the suite a deliverable in its own right is the goal change, and its home is docs/anchors.md. Register it there, because the registry is the only place that survives a rename and is the only one a machine checks.

The strongest argument against B — and the answer

Against: you have one live product that has not shipped in 52 days; promoting its scaffolding to a deliverable is how a project ends up admiring its own instruments. Answer: that is an argument about sequencing, not about the registry. Registering an anchor costs one line and schedules nothing — and leaving it unregistered is what let the altitude gap in §03 survive five weeks. Register it, then decide what to work on next, which is a separate question I am not asking you here.
