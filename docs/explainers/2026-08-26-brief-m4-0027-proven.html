The decision: how much review before the PR opens

Nine commits and 3 047 insertions have landed since the round-9 review halves were written — including the whole of T1, T2, T4, T5 and the migration itself. No review round has seen any of it.

Recommended

Finish T7–T10, then one review round over the whole branch, then PR

T7 and T8 modify the same gate infrastructure a review would examine — T7 adds the behavioural suite to the gate list, T8 rehomes 05_assert.sql. Reviewing now means reviewing that surface twice, and the second pass invalidates the first.

B

Round 10 now, on the nine unreviewed commits

Smaller batch, earlier feedback. Costs a second round later over T7–T10.

C

Open the PR now and review in it

Fastest to a visible artifact; leaves T7–T10 as follow-ups and the milestone gate unmet.

The strongest argument against the recommendation, stated at full weight: this project has measured (portable-practices §12) that late-round defects are mostly caused by the previous round's fix. A larger unreviewed batch is a larger surface for exactly that, and by T10 it would be roughly 4 000+ lines rather than 3 000 — reviewed once, by reviewers who cannot hold it all at once.

The answer: that risk is about fixes stacking unreviewed, and the branch is currently green on every runnable gate plus both live-gate polarities — so the batch is not a pile of unverified changes, it is a pile of mechanically verified ones. The defect class §12 warns about is instrument self-damage, and that is now the best-covered thing here: 58 schema mutations, 29 live-gate mutations, a premise gate, and T5's ratchets which proved themselves by catching their own staleness one commit after being repaired. If you disagree with that reasoning, option B is the safe call and I would take it without argument.
