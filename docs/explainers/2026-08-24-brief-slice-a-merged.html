Where I got it wrong

Every defect today was about the layer, not the logic — including mine

  1. “Docker is down.” Asserted from one hung docker info. The daemon was wedged. Then I corrected it with nc -z, which proves a listener accepts, not that a database answers — the port-forwarder outlives the container. Wrong twice, opposite directions.
  2. The mutation harness silently no-opped. psql -f /tmp/… reads the container's filesystem. M1 never applied and printed Tests: 8 passed — byte-identical to a surviving mutation.
  3. The containment assertion was never reached. Under M1 the falsifier failed on the rejection half, so jest stopped and the ledger line — the reason (b′) exists — was never evaluated. The plan predicted this in writing.
  4. I merged #134 on a red check. I recognised the error in the log as one I had just fixed and called the run stale. It was stale — and the fix was not in the PR. “Stale failure” and “real failure” are indistinguishable when the fix lives only in your working tree.
  5. And the red check was hiding a second failure. CI stops at the first failing step, so the architecture ratchet had never run on that branch. Slice A had pushed three findings past baseline — an inline backend read, a 12th UUID_RE, a 9th local/cloud fork, all with target zero. Repaired by making the route thin: the handler moved to lib/, two shared helpers extracted.
  6. I ran five ratchets and there are six. check-arch-findings is in dev-process.md's enforced table and was not in my habit.

The decision

Merge #135, then apply 0026 to prod and re-run the anon check before deploying.

No design question is open. The feature is verified to a depth prose cannot reach: the SQL has executed, and the one claim the whole (b′) redesign exists to make is now provably load-bearing rather than merely written down.

The strongest argument against

Four of my instruments were wrong today and I merged on a red check. If the tests were that unreliable, why trust the greens? A suite whose author kept mis-measuring is not obviously evidence of anything.

The answer

Every one of those four was caught by running something, and each was a test that could not see its subject — not a test that reported the wrong verdict about a subject it did see. The greens that remain are different in kind: the SQL executed, the mutation is confirmed present in the catalog before the verdict is believed, and the ledger delta was read back as a number. The honest reading is not “trust the suite” but “trust the three facts that were read out of a live database.”
