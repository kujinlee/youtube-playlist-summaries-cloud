1What you asked, and what the spec turns it into

Your words: “Now is the time to evaluate the efforts… Not all of them may not be suitable for publish but let's take inventory and establish a milestone.”

The spec turns that into one question asked of every component: could someone who has never seen this repository install it and use it? Three efforts, 24 components, four criteria, three permitted verdicts.

Why this has never been answered before

Each of the three efforts was justified on its own terms — fewer review rounds, cheaper sweeps, pages you can follow. None was ever asked whether it travels. That question is Deliverable 2's entire content, and it has been answered informally, for one file, once.

2The four decisions — and the case against each

You took these by selection card today. The spec records them as settled, which is only safe if you have seen what each one rules out. Every one of them is still reversible right now.

D1 · AUDIENCE Public strangers, full support bar. Works in any repo, documented for a newcomer, failure modes handled without you.

This is the expensive one, and it changes the other three. Coupling stops being untidiness and becomes a release blocker. It adds newcomer documentation, failure handling, and an ongoing support burden for tools whose real user is you.

The case against

Most of effort (2) — CI sharding, a mutation harness wired to this repo's workflow — is unlikely to clear this bar at all, so the bar may simply convert that effort into a DON'T-SHIP you could have predicted for free. And the support burden is real work that competes with the product, which has just had its first deploy in 52 days.

What would make the softer option right: if you do not actually intend to tell anyone about this, or if the support burden would stop the work rather than shape it. Then “install it into my own next project” is the honest target, and the bar drops a long way.

D2 · DECOMPOSITION One evaluation across all three efforts, then a spec per publishable unit.

One PR produces the inventory and the milestone; the publishing work comes after, per surviving unit.

The case against

Effort (3) is the obvious candidate — it is already shaped like a plugin, and its coupling is already measured and concentrated in one file. Evaluating (1) and (2) to the same depth may be work spent confirming a foregone conclusion, which is the most expensive kind of thoroughness.

What would make going straight at (3) right: if you are already confident that (1) and (2) are DON'T-SHIP. Then the spec should say so now and spend the effort on making (3) genuinely installable instead. This is question 2 at the bottom.

D3 · ROUTE A self-published marketplace repo, installed with /plugin marketplace add <repo>.

Proven rather than theorised: ~/.claude/plugins/marketplaces/ on this machine holds omc, a third-party self-published marketplace, beside the official one. A marketplace is a git repo carrying .claude-plugin/marketplace.json.

The case against

Discovery is nil. Nobody finds a self-published marketplace by browsing; “published” here means “installable by anyone you tell”. If the point of publishing is that people encounter it, this route does not deliver that.

What would make the official list right: if discovery matters more to you than controlling the ship date. The cost is an acceptance gate on an unknown timeline — which makes “done” undefinable, and is why the spec rules it out as a definition of done rather than as a destination. It stays available as a later, separate milestone.

D4 · METHOD A mechanical scan across all components, plus one real install experiment on the comprehensibility suite, where the experiment calibrates the scan.

The scan counts what is countable. The install experiment is the falsifier: any blocker it hits on a component the scan scored clean is a scanner defect, fixed in the scan — not recorded as a footnote.

The case against

The install experiment costs real time and will fail noisily several times before it tells you anything. A scan alone would land the inventory far sooner.

What would make scan-only right: if criterion C4 (supportable) does not really matter. But that contradicts D1 — a public bar is mostly C4. The two decisions are coupled: soften D1 and D4 becomes optional; keep D1 and D4 is the only thing that tests it.

The interaction worth noticing

D1 and D4 hold each other up, and D1 and D2 pull against each other. The public bar is what makes the install experiment necessary — and it is also what makes evaluating effort (2) likely to be a formality. If you were to soften any one of these, soften D1 first, because the other three mostly follow from it.

5Where this plan is weakest

Three things you should see rather than be protected from.

1 · The install experiment covers effort (3) only

Efforts (1) and (2) get verdicts from C1–C3 plus argument — which is exactly the judgement-based evaluation that D4 exists to avoid. The spec states this as a bound rather than hiding it, and requires those verdicts be labelled as argument-based. It is still an asymmetry, and it is question 1 below.

2 · The spec was written with an invented number

It said “~13 components”. The derived figure is 24. Nothing depended on 13 — that is the point: a number with no provenance walked straight into a design document whose own risk table names that exact drift.

Caught by the spec's self-review and recorded in its §9b rather than quietly fixed. It is evidence for the mechanical scan in §5 of the spec, not against it.

3 · Nothing is installable at the end of this milestone

PR 1 produces no plugin, no marketplace repo, and fixes no coupling — not even brief/SKILL.md's nine known references. Its entire output is a decision you can trust, plus the scan that keeps it honest.

That was deliberate: fixing a component while also judging it is how this project has previously manufactured a false green. If you expected something shippable from this milestone, say so now — it is a different plan, not a tweak to this one.

7Four things I would like you to weigh

1 · Is the lopsided evidence acceptable?

Effort (3) gets a real install experiment. Efforts (1) and (2) get argument. My recommendation: accept it — an install experiment for a CI harness and a memory matcher would cost more than their likely verdicts are worth.

The counter is sharp, though: it means the two efforts you have spent the most recent worry on get the weakest evidence, and their verdicts will be mine rather than measured. If that is not acceptable, the fix is one experiment each, and it roughly doubles the milestone.

2 · Is effort (2) worth evaluating at all?

CI sharding and the mutation harness are wired into this repository's workflow. Under D1's public bar I expect most of it to come out DON'T-SHIP. My recommendation: evaluate it anyway, because “I expect” is the kind of claim this project keeps finding wrong, and the scan is cheap once written.

The counter: if the answer really is foregone, the spec should say so now and spend the effort on making effort (3) installable instead. You have better information than I do about whether the sharding idea is worth anything to someone else — it is a technique, not a tool, and techniques travel as prose.

3 · Should the stale index claims be fixed here or separately?

Three claims in portable-practices.md are wrong — the size, the anchor count, and an owed anchor that now exists. The spec puts the corrections inside PR 1. My recommendation: keep them here, because the inventory is what discovered them and a correction separated from its evidence tends not to land.

The counter: it mixes a factual repair into a decision document, and if the milestone stalls, three known-wrong sentences stay published while it does.

4 · Does “publishable” need a support policy decided first?

D1 commits to a public bar, which implies versioning, breaking-change handling, and answering strangers' issues. The spec does not price any of that, and the inventory will happily mark things SHIP without it.

My recommendation: decide it before the inventory, not after — a component is only shippable against a stated support commitment, and “we will work that out later” is how an open-source burden gets acquired by accident. But this is genuinely your call: you are the one who would carry it.
