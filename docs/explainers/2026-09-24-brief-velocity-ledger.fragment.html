1 · The spine: review was a generator, not a sieve

The natural assumption about a proposal going through review is that review removes things — the document proposes ten, six survive. That is not what happened, and noticing it is the difference between reading the ledger and understanding it.

PR #345 moved in both directions:

DirectionWhatWhy
DROPPED The literal wording “a side job re-asks Q0”, which §7 proposed Q0 does not exist. A rule pointing at nothing is a rule that cannot run, which this repo treats as a failure rather than a pass. What landed instead is the part that is true without Q0, with the attachment point marked for when Q0 arrives.
ADDED Rule 1b — say what you counted, not just that you counted Not in the proposal at all. It was produced by §6’s own first review round, from three defects in the change that adopted §6. The document now says so: development-velocity.md:197 — “1b did not exist when §6 was written; it was produced by §6’s own first review round.”
ADDED The shape invariant — a scope plus three refused forms Also absent from the proposal. It was written at round 4 after four pattern-shaped fixes each failed to terminate.

So the ledger has three columns, not two. “Proposed and adopted”, “proposed and not adopted”, and “not proposed, and adopted anyway” — the last of which is where the two most load-bearing rules came from.

They are a list, not a count — deliberately

The section preamble (process-checklists.md:452) reads:

They are numbered 1, 1b, 2, 3 and 4 — listed, not counted, and that is deliberate.

Because the count went stale twice: “all four” when 1b was added, and then the repair re-pinned it as “five of them”. A list cannot disagree with its own contents.

The shape invariant

Added at round 4, after four pattern-shaped fixes failed to terminate (process-checklists.md:569):

Scope: this section — its preamble and the bodies of rules 1, 1b, 2, 3 and 4.
Within it, three forms are refused:

  1. a count of anything in this repository, in digits or in words —
     including a count of this section's own contents;
  2. a file:line locator where the name of the symbol, constant or
     comment would serve;
  3. a document-relative position — "twenty lines below", "the section above".

Everything else stays, including quotations of code and cross-references by name.

The enumeration is the test; the prose sentence is only its reason. An earlier version led with “makes no claim about the repository’s contents” — and read as the test, that clause forbids three expressions these rules need, including rule 3’s cross-reference. A criterion that condemns the repo’s own standing rule about quoting code is the wrong criterion.

Rule 3 is a pointer, not a copy

It defers to review-method.md §0 Q2 step 5 rather than restating it (process-checklists.md:526): “That step binds the reviewer; nothing bound the author, and the two findings above are what that gap produced.” The reviewer-side rule already existed; what was missing was anything binding the author.

4 · Trap: rule 1 and rule 1b are different rules

They look like one rule split in two. They are not, and conflating them is how three of this branch’s defects happened.

Rule 1 — provenanceRule 1b — population
AsksDid you measure it?Does the label name what the command counted?
Fails whenThe number was recalled or inferredThe number was measured, and names a different set
Example“the draft PR caught that Blocking” — no measurement existed“28 gates” — 28 was real; gates was not what the regex counted

A number can pass rule 1 and fail rule 1b, and that is not a corner case — it is what happened three times in the change that introduced the rule. All three answered yes to “did you measure it”.

6 · Trap: §5’s headline number is cracked

§5 argues the draft-PR practice with (development-velocity.md:142):

GitHub is roughly TWICE as fast as this machine: measured on PR #342, the
verify job completed in 8m08s including the sweep, tsc, the unit suite and
~30 other gates. The local sweep alone takes ~14 minutes.

Two problems, and the first is what rule 1b exists for.

  1. Different populations. It compares CI’s whole verify job against the local sweep alone — the CI side doing strictly more work. The honest comparison is sweep-to-sweep.
  2. The denominator moved. verify was 488s on PR #342 and is 673–680s now — about 39% slower in three days.

⚠ The corrected ratio is NOT KNOWN. The local sweep was not re-run on this tree, so this page gives you the crack, not the fix. What is measured is only that the CI side grew and the comparison was between unlike things to begin with.

Where CI time actually goes

Measured from the Actions API across two successful runs:

StepTimeShare
Mutation sweep (--mutate .)554s / 542s~81%
Unit + component tests (2,892)27s / 26s~4%
Everything else (tsc, installs, ~30 guards)~95s~15%
verify total680s / 673s
schema-gates (concurrent workflow)103s0 wall clock

Each mutation re-runs its target’s entire --self-test in a fresh interpreter (scripts/check-plan-code.py:501), 1,030 times. That isolation is why the sweep is slow — and it is also what keeps a mutation’s verdict honest, so it is a cost rather than waste. §5’s four items all optimise frequency, because cost is load-bearing.
